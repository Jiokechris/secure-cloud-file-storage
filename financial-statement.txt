Financial Accounting: Core Concepts and Principles
Financial accounting is the structured process of recording, summarizing, and reporting a business's financial transactions to provide external stakeholders—such as investors, creditors, regulators, and tax authorities—with an accurate picture of its financial health and operational performance.

1. Core Framework and Principles
Financial accounting relies on Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) or International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) to maintain consistency and comparability across organizations.

Accrual Basis: Revenue is recognized when earned, and expenses are recognized when incurred, regardless of when cash changes hands.

Double-Entry Bookkeeping: Every transaction affects at least two accounts (one debit, one credit), keeping the core balance sheet equation in balance.

Matching Principle: Expenses must be matched directly with the revenues they helped generate during the same accounting period.

2. The Core Accounting Equation
Assets=Liabilities+Equity
Assets: Economic resources owned or controlled by the enterprise (e.g., cash, accounts receivable, equipment).

Liabilities: Financial obligations or debts owed to external parties (e.g., accounts payable, loans).

Equity: The residual interest in the assets after deducting liabilities, representing the owners' stake.

3. Key Financial Statements
Statement	Primary Objective	Key Components
Balance Sheet	Measures financial position at a specific point in time.	Assets, Liabilities, Shareholders' Equity
Income Statement	Measures financial performance over a defined period.	Revenues, Cost of Goods Sold (COGS), Operating Expenses, Net Income
Cash Flow Statement	Tracks cash inflows and outflows over a period.	Operating Activities, Investing Activities, Financing Activities
Statement of Equity	Tracks changes in owner's equity over a period.	Common Stock, Retained Earnings, Dividends
4. The Accounting Cycle
[ Transactions ] ──> [ Journal Entries ] ──> [ General Ledger ]
                                                     │
[ Financial Statements ] <── [ Adjusted Trial Balance ] ◄──┘
Identify and Analyze Transactions: Gathering receipts, invoices, and source documents.

Journalize: Recording initial entries chronologically via debits and credits.

Post to General Ledger: Transferring entries to individual account ledgers.

Adjusting Entries: Accruing unbilled revenues/expenses and adjusting for prepayments or depreciation.

Close the Books: Transferring temporary income/expense account balances into retained earnings at year-end.
